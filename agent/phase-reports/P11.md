### 2026-09-28 — native JFK-absence defect: root cause found and fixed in source

The most valuable observation in this log is that native UI reached a real
microphone and local ASR twice while the expected JFK fragment was absent both
times. That was never a flaky microphone or a flaky model. It was a real,
deterministic defect in the endpointing layer, in `NativeEnergyVad` and
`DictationEndpointConfig` in `crates/narrivy-core/src/endpoint.rs`. All three
causes are now fixed in source, with a regression test each.

**1. Absolute VAD thresholds sat far above a real microphone's noise floor.**
`EnergyVadConfig` at commit `14c89af` used fixed absolute levels —
`activation_rms: 0.015`, `release_rms: 0.008`, `activation_peak: 0.04`,
`release_peak: 0.02` — and the module had no notion of a room-tone floor.
Measured window RMS for real room tone is ~0.0085–0.0124 on archival tape
material and ~0.0006–0.0014 on a quiet-room USB condenser, a ~14x spread. The
old activation threshold of 0.015 therefore sat 10.7x above the loudest
measured quiet-room floor and 25x above the quietest, so on a real USB
microphone it sat above the speech it was supposed to detect. The fix replaces
absolute levels with ratios against a tracked room-tone floor, and the ratio
rationale and the measured spread are now documented in the type at
`crates/narrivy-core/src/endpoint.rs:51` with defaults at
`crates/narrivy-core/src/endpoint.rs:79`. The floor follows the quietest window
of each 1-second block rather than speech level
(`crates/narrivy-core/src/endpoint.rs:196`). Regressions:
`energy_vad_floor_adapts_across_a_wide_noise_level_range`
(`crates/narrivy-core/src/endpoint.rs:604`) probes 0.0008, 0.0085 and 0.012
floors and requires 12x-above-floor speech to read as speech at every one of
them; `energy_vad_hysteresis_prevents_boundary_flapping`
(`crates/narrivy-core/src/endpoint.rs:628`) pins the hysteresis to the ratios
rather than to a drifting floor.

**2. Classification was not chunk-partition invariant.** At `14c89af` the VAD
computed one RMS over the whole `AudioChunk` and returned exactly one
`SpeechActivity` for it, so the activity sequence was a function of how the
capture transport happened to split the byte stream, not of the audio. A pause
inside a chunk was averaged away. The fix classifies per fixed 20 ms
`ACTIVITY_WINDOW_MS` analysis windows and returns every window a chunk
completed, so the decision sequence is a property of the audio alone
(`crates/narrivy-core/src/endpoint.rs:24`,
`crates/narrivy-core/src/endpoint.rs:29`,
`crates/narrivy-core/src/endpoint.rs:258`). Regression:
`energy_vad_decisions_are_invariant_to_chunk_partitioning`
(`crates/narrivy-core/src/endpoint.rs:647`) replays identical audio at seven
chunk sizes from 160 to 2,560 frames and requires an identical activity
sequence; `endpoint_duration_is_invariant_to_chunk_partitioning`
(`crates/narrivy-core/src/endpoint.rs:860`) does the same for the finalize
boundary at 640, 1,280 and 2,560 frames.

**3. The hang-over cut clause pauses in half.** `DictationEndpointConfig` at
`14c89af` used `trailing_silence_ms: 560`. The pinned qualification fixture's
own intra-utterance pauses run 1.08s and 1.14s
(`crates/narrivy-core/src/endpoint.rs:788`), so a 560 ms hang-over finalized
mid-sentence at every natural clause boundary and the transcriber never saw the
remainder of the JFK fragment. The default is now `trailing_silence_ms: 1_500`
(`crates/narrivy-core/src/endpoint.rs:325`), above the longest observed pause.
Regression: `endpoint_does_not_finalize_on_a_natural_clause_pause`
(`crates/narrivy-core/src/endpoint.rs:794`) asserts the hang-over is at least
1,200 ms and that a 1.12s pause followed by resumed speech returns
`EndpointDecision::Continue`.

`cargo test --locked -p narrivy-core endpoint` passes 15 tests including all of
the above. This is source acceptance for a defect that produced a real,
repeated native failure; it is not native qualification. The native
known-phrase lane has not been re-run against an exact clean package, so
`TEST-010` stays blocked and the release stays red.
